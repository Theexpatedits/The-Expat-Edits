VIETNAM · OUR EDITION

Hanoi & Bai Tu Long Bay

Rooftops, old streets & the quieter side of the bay.

OUR HANOI & BAI TU LONG BAY

Always look up. Then slow right down.

Hanoi was a brilliant city to explore on foot. We loved the energy of the Old Quarter, the flower sellers, the temples and the constant movement of the streets. There was always something happening, and just as often something worth stopping for.

We seemed to spend half our time looking upwards for the next rooftop bar or restaurant. Then we left the city behind for two days aboard the Prince with Indochina Boats, cruising Bai Tu Long Bay — a quieter stretch of water where limestone cliffs rise out of emerald green.

WHERE WE STAYED

La Sinfonía del Rey Hotel & Spa.

OUR HANOI BASE

A superb position by the lake.

La Sinfonía del Rey was a brilliant choice for our stay in Hanoi. The location was superb: right by Hoan Kiem Lake, with the Old Quarter, restaurants, cafés and the life of the city all within easy walking distance.

The rooms were lovely and well appointed. We had a balcony overlooking the lake, which gave us a wonderful place to pause and take in the view after a day exploring. The hotel was also kind enough to upgrade the children's room, which made the stay that bit more special.

One of the highlights was the rooftop bar. We enjoyed heading up there for sunset, looking out across the rooftops and the lake as the light changed over the city.

The spa was another welcome addition, with excellent massages after long days on our feet. All in all, a very comfortable hotel in exactly the right spot for discovering Hanoi.

A hotel we would happily recommend as a base for exploring the city, particularly if being close to the lake and the Old Quarter matters to you.

THE LOCATION

Step outside and find yourself in the middle of it.

We loved being able to wander around the lake and then disappear into the surrounding streets. Hanoi is a city best explored without too much of a plan: follow the food, the flowers, the cafés and whatever catches your eye.

THE CITY

Hanoi rewards the curious.

THE OLD QUARTER

A city that keeps moving.

Produce spills onto the pavement, scooters thread their way through the streets and flower sellers bring colour to the everyday bustle. We loved simply wandering and seeing where the streets took us.

HIDDEN DETAILS

Look beyond the bustle.

Ornate gateways, temple details and unexpected pockets of greenery sit alongside the city's constant movement. We enjoyed taking our time and noticing the smaller things.

THE EVERYDAY

Street life, up close.

Some of our favourite moments came from watching daily life unfold: a market stall, a shopfront, a small café or a street that looked worth exploring.

OUT ON THE WATER

Bai Tu Long Bay. A slower kind of spectacular.

THE PRINCE

Two days aboard the bay.

After the energy of Hanoi, heading out onto the water felt like stepping into another world. We spent two days aboard the Prince with Indochina Boats, cruising through the limestone scenery of Bai Tu Long Bay.

THE LANDSCAPE

Room to take it all in.

The scenery was extraordinary: steep limestone cliffs, little passages of open water and islands appearing around every bend. We were really pleased to have chosen this quieter part of the bay.

LIFE ON BOARD

A beach, a canoe and nowhere else to be.

We found a lovely quiet beach and went canoeing straight from the boat. The food was plentiful, the views kept changing and the days took on that rare feeling of having nowhere else to be.

ONE LAST MEMORY

Sleeping under the stars. Almost.

I even tried sleeping out on deck under the stars. It was a lovely idea until the rain arrived and sent me back to the cabin. Still, it made for a memorable end to our time on the water.

WHEN TO GO

Choose the season

Northern Vietnam has distinct seasonal changes. Check conditions for your dates, particularly before an overnight cruise, when weather can affect the experience on the water.

THE FEEL

Two different rhythms

Expect busy streets, rooftop terraces and a lively food scene in Hanoi, followed by quiet coves, emerald water and dramatic limestone scenery in Bai Tu Long Bay.

GETTING AROUND

On foot and by boat

Hanoi's central sights are easy to explore on foot. For the bay, arrange the transfer with your cruise operator and allow the boat journey to set the pace.

OUR RECOMMENDATIONS

Places we loved.

01

La Sinfonía del Rey Hotel & Spa

A superbly located base by Hoan Kiem Lake, with lovely rooms, our lake-view balcony, a rooftop bar for sunsets and an excellent spa. The team kindly upgraded the children's room after our website outreach.

02

Hanoi's Old Quarter

Come ready to wander. The streets are full of small discoveries, from food and flowers to shopfronts, architecture and the everyday theatre of city life.

03

Hoan Kiem Lake

A lovely counterpoint to the surrounding streets. Walk the lake edge, take in the red bridge and enjoy a little breathing space in the middle of the city.

04

Blank

A really good spot for food and atmosphere, with a live DJ and the sort of lively energy that makes you want to stay a little longer.

05

Hanoi Garden

A lovely courtyard restaurant and a great place for the kids to try Vietnamese food in a relaxed setting.

06

Saha

A lovely little coffee shop near the hotel, and a good place to try local egg coffee.

07

Prince by Indochina Boats

Our two days cruising Bai Tu Long Bay included extraordinary limestone scenery, a quiet beach, canoeing and plentiful food — a memorable way to experience the quieter side of the bay.

08

Hanoi Train Street

A memorable stop back in the city. The kids put bottle caps on the tracks and brought home a flattened souvenir after the train had passed.

FROM THE JOURNAL

A trip of two very different rhythms.

In Hanoi, we were always looking up for the next rooftop, finding a good meal or stopping for egg coffee. Blank brought food and a live DJ; Hanoi Garden gave us a lovely courtyard setting; and Saha was a good excuse to stop for a local coffee. Our hotel put us right in the middle of it all, with the lake just outside and sunsets waiting on the rooftop.

Then came two days aboard the Prince in Bai Tu Long Bay: quiet beaches, canoeing, plentiful food and limestone cliffs rising out of emerald water. My attempt to sleep under the stars ended with a retreat to the cabin in the rain. Back in Hanoi, Train Street and the kids' flattened bottle-cap souvenir brought the trip back to street level.

OUR MAP

Hanoi & Bai Tu Long Bay.

From our base beside Hoan Kiem Lake to the quieter waters of Bai Tu Long Bay, this was a trip of contrasts — and one we'll remember for its food, family moments, rooftop sunsets and extraordinary scenery.
