VIETNAM

Ho Chi Minh City

A weekend in the heart of Saigon.

OUR HO CHI MINH CITY

A city that makes you want to keep walking.

We spent a weekend exploring Ho Chi Minh City on foot, moving through District 1, stopping whenever something caught our eye and gradually getting pulled into the extraordinary rhythm of Saigon. It is busy, noisy and occasionally chaotic — but somehow it all works.

The Central Post Office and Independence Palace gave us glimpses of the city's history, while shops, street food, cafés and Nguyen Hue kept drawing us back into the energy of modern Saigon.

Ho Chi Minh City, as we see it.

THE CITY

Saigon reveals itself one street at a time.

We quickly realised that the best way to experience Ho Chi Minh City was simply to walk. District 1 gave us the perfect starting point — shops, cafés, restaurants, old buildings and an almost constant stream of life.

THE OLD SAIGON

History sits comfortably among the chaos.

The Saigon Central Post Office was one of those places that stopped us in our tracks. Its grand architecture feels completely different from the streets outside, yet somehow belongs to the same city.

Independence Palace offered another glimpse into the city's past. Walking between these landmarks gave the weekend a useful sense of perspective — modern Saigon moving at full speed around pieces of its history.

DISTRICT 1

Walk first. Decide where you're going later.

District 1 became our natural playground. We wandered between shops, cafés and restaurants, stopping for food whenever we fancied it and letting the streets dictate the route.

AFTER DARK

Then the city changes gear.

As evening arrived, Nguyen Hue Walking Street became one of our favourite places to be. The crowds gathered, the lights came on and the energy seemed to rise another level.

And everywhere around it was the traffic — an extraordinary river of motorbikes flowing through the city. At first it looked completely chaotic. After a while, you started to realise there was an almost perfect choreography to it.

FROM THE JOURNAL

Ho Chi Minh City makes you want to keep moving.

There are cities where you need a plan. Ho Chi Minh City wasn't one of them. We spent the weekend walking, following whatever looked interesting and letting the city gradually reveal itself.

District 1 became our starting point, with its shops, restaurants and cafés. The Central Post Office and Independence Palace added a completely different layer, reminders that beneath the modern energy is a city with an extraordinary history.

Street food became part of the rhythm. So did shopping, stopping to watch the traffic and simply taking in the incredible amount of life happening around us.

By evening, Nguyen Hue was buzzing. The crowds had gathered and the city seemed to turn the volume up. Motorbikes streamed through the streets in seemingly impossible numbers, somehow avoiding one another with remarkable precision.

That was our Ho Chi Minh City: walk, eat, look around, stop, start again. A short weekend that somehow felt completely full.
