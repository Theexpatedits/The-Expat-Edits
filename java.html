SOUTH EAST ASIA

Java

Temples, volcanoes, coffee & a slower pace.

OUR JAVA

A slower side of Indonesia.

We began our Java journey at Borobudur, arriving before sunrise for an early morning tour. It was the ideal way to experience the temple — quiet, atmospheric and gradually appearing as the light came up around it.

From there we headed north, moving away from the temple and into a much greener and quieter part of Java. We stayed at Le Temple, which was fine, although after enjoying a couple of lunches at Plataran, that would be our choice if we returned.

We also had an exceptional lunch at Amanjiwo. It is certainly a splurge, but if the budget stretches that far, it is a wonderful experience.

From there we drove towards MesaStila, where the pace slowed even further. The coffee plantation, hammam spa, surrounding mountains, tiered paddy fields and vegetable gardens became some of our favourite parts of the journey.

Java, as we saw it.

THE BEGINNING

Borobudur before the day begins.

Arriving before sunrise was the perfect way to experience Borobudur. The temple slowly appeared as the light changed, creating a quiet and atmospheric beginning to the trip.

What we loved almost as much was seeing it again afterwards, appearing in the distance as we travelled through the countryside.

ON THE ROAD

Leaving the temple behind.

From Borobudur we headed north, away from the temple and into a greener, quieter part of Java.

Having a driver made the journey between places simple and allowed us to enjoy the scenery rather than rush through it.

THE COUNTRYSIDE

The journey became part of the experience.

The further we travelled, the more Java opened up — villages, fields, mountains and a landscape that seemed to change around every bend.

PLACES ALONG THE WAY

Long lunches & beautiful settings.

We stayed at Le Temple, which was perfectly fine, but a couple of lunches at Plataran changed our minds about where we would stay next time.

OUR EDIT

Plataran & Amanjiwo.

Plataran is where we would stay if we returned to the area. We loved the setting and enjoyed a couple of lunches there.

We also had an exceptional lunch at Amanjiwo. It is certainly a splurge, but if your budget stretches that far, it is a wonderful experience.

TOWARDS MESASTILA

The landscape gets greener.

From Amanjiwo we drove towards MesaStila and the pace slowed again. The mountains became more prominent and the landscape increasingly rural.

MESASTILA

Into coffee country.

SLOWER MOMENTS

MesaStila changed the pace.

MesaStila was one of our highlights. Walking through the coffee plantation, enjoying the hammam spa and trekking into the surrounding mountains gave us a completely different perspective on Indonesia.

THE LANDSCAPE

Green in every direction.

Tiered paddy fields and vegetable gardens filled the landscape, while the volcanoes seemed to be everywhere.

OUR FAVOURITE EXPERIENCE

Walk the plantation.

One of our favourite experiences at MesaStila was simply walking among the coffee plants. It gave us a real sense of the landscape and the way the area lives.

THE LANDSCAPE

Coffee, fields & mountains.

COFFEE COUNTRY

Java at its most peaceful.

The coffee plantation was one of the places where the slower rhythm of the trip really made sense. There was nowhere we needed to be quickly.

RURAL JAVA

Fields and gardens.

The tiered fields and vegetable gardens were stunning. They made the scale of the surrounding mountains even more impressive.

THE VOLCANOES

Impossible to ignore.

The volcanoes were a constant presence — beautiful from the ground and one of the defining features of the landscape.

THE VOLCANOES

The mountains are always there.

THE SCALE OF JAVA

Beautiful from the ground.

The volcanoes seemed to appear everywhere as we moved through the countryside. Sometimes distant beyond the fields, sometimes dominating the horizon.

OUR EXPERIENCE

Java rewards slowing down.

More than anything, we loved the feeling of Java. It is laid back, green and unhurried, and that slower pace made the whole trip feel very different from some of our other travels around Asia.

FROM THE AIR

Java looks different from above.

THE LAST LOOK

The view on the way home.

We had been surrounded by volcanoes and mountains for much of the trip, but seeing them again from the plane windows gave us a final reminder of just how extraordinary the landscape is.

It was a fitting end to a journey that had begun at one of Indonesia's great temples and gradually taken us into the countryside, plantations and mountains beyond.

BEST TIME

May – October

The drier months are generally the easiest time to explore Java, particularly if you want to combine temples, walking and mountain experiences.

WEATHER

Tropical

Warm conditions at lower elevations with cooler temperatures as you climb into the mountains. The landscape is beautifully green.

GETTING AROUND

Driver & road

Having a driver made the journey between Borobudur, the plantations and mountain areas simple and allowed us to enjoy the scenery along the way.

OUR EDIT

Slow down and look around.

Java isn't a destination we'd rush through. The best part of our trip was the journey between places — watching Borobudur appear through the countryside, driving towards the mountains and seeing the landscape gradually change.

Leave some space in the itinerary. Walk the plantation, take the long lunch, explore the countryside and look out of the plane window on the way home. Java rewards slowing down.
