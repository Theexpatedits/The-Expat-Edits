SOUTH EAST ASIA

Java

Temples, volcanoes, coffee & a slower pace.

OUR JAVA

A slower side of Indonesia.

Java wasn't really about ticking places off a list. It was about slowing down and watching the landscape change around us — from one of Indonesia's great temples to coffee plantations, mountains and fields disappearing into the distance.

We began at Borobudur, arriving before sunrise for an early morning tour. The temple gradually appeared as the light came up, and that quiet beginning set the tone for much of the journey that followed.

From there we headed north, away from the temple and into a greener, quieter part of Java. We stayed at Le Temple, which was perfectly fine, although after visiting Plataran for lunch we decided that would be our choice if we returned.

THE BEGINNING

Borobudur before the day begins.

BOROBUDUR

Worth getting up for.

Arriving before sunrise was the perfect way to experience Borobudur. The temple was quiet and atmospheric, slowly emerging as the light changed around it.

What stayed with us just as much was seeing the temple afterwards, appearing again and again in the distance as we travelled through the countryside. Borobudur felt less like an isolated sight and more like the beginning of the journey.

ON THE ROAD

Leaving the temple behind.

THE JOURNEY

The best part was often between the places.

From Borobudur we headed north, and the character of the trip began to change. The roads took us away from the temple and deeper into a landscape of green hills, villages and mountains.

Having a driver made the journey simple. There was no need to rush between destinations, and we could spend the time looking out of the window and watching Java unfold.

PLACES ALONG THE WAY

Long lunches & beautiful settings.

OUR EDIT

Two places worth making time for.

We stayed at Le Temple, which was perfectly fine, but a couple of lunches at Plataran changed our minds about where we would stay next time. We loved the setting and would choose it if we returned.

We also had an exceptional lunch at Amanjiwo. It is certainly a splurge, but if your budget stretches that far, it is a wonderful experience — and one that fits perfectly into a slower Java itinerary.

MESASTILA

Into coffee country.

SLOWER MOMENTS

MesaStila changed the pace.

From Amanjiwo we drove towards MesaStila, and the trip slowed down again. This was one of our favourite parts of Java.

Walking through the coffee plantation, spending time in the hammam spa and trekking into the surrounding mountains gave us a completely different perspective on Indonesia.

The landscape here was extraordinary. Tiered paddy fields and vegetable gardens filled the valleys, while the volcanoes seemed to be everywhere around us.

THE LANDSCAPE

Green in every direction.

OUR FAVOURITE EXPERIENCE

Walk the plantation.

One of our favourite experiences at MesaStila was simply walking through the coffee plantation. It made the surrounding landscape feel much more tangible — not just something beautiful to look at, but somewhere people work and live.

This was the Java we enjoyed most: quiet paths, mountains in the distance, fields below and very little reason to hurry.

RURAL JAVA

Fields, gardens & volcanoes.

THE VOLCANOES

They are impossible to ignore.

The volcanoes became a constant presence as we moved through the countryside. Sometimes they were distant shapes beyond the fields; at other times they seemed to rise directly out of the landscape.

They were one of the things that made Java feel so different from the other places we had travelled in Indonesia. The scale of the landscape was always there, even when the pace of the journey was slow.

FROM THE AIR

Java looks different from above.

THE LAST LOOK

The view on the way home.

We had been surrounded by the volcanoes and mountains for much of the trip, but seeing them again from the plane windows gave us a final reminder of just how extraordinary the landscape is.

It was a fitting end to a journey that had started at one of Indonesia's great temples and gradually taken us into the countryside, plantations and mountains beyond.

BEST TIME

May – October

The drier months are generally the easiest time to explore Java, particularly if you want to combine temples, walking and mountain experiences.

WEATHER

Tropical

Warm conditions at lower elevations with cooler temperatures as you climb into the mountains. The landscape is beautifully green.

GETTING AROUND

Driver & road

Having a driver made the journey between Borobudur, the plantations and mountain areas simple and allowed us to enjoy the scenery along the way.

OUR EDIT

Slow down and look around.

Java isn't a destination we'd rush through. The best part of our trip was the journey between places — watching Borobudur appear through the countryside, driving towards the mountains and seeing the landscape gradually change.

Leave some space in the itinerary. Walk the plantation, take the long lunch, explore the countryside and look out of the plane window on the way home. Java rewards slowing down.
