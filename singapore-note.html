EDITOR'S NOTE

3 Days in Singapore

Our way of spending a long weekend in the city.

Singapore is one of those cities that manages to feel both incredibly cosmopolitan and surprisingly relaxed. You can spend the morning wandering through tropical gardens, have lunch beside a swimming pool, watch the sun disappear behind one of the world's most recognisable skylines and finish the evening somewhere with a little atmosphere.

It is also a wonderfully easy place to visit. English is widely spoken, getting around is straightforward and there is an enormous choice of hotels, restaurants and things to do. You can pack plenty into a short stay without feeling that you need to rush from one attraction to another.

Having made Singapore home, we have come to appreciate that the best days here are often a mixture of doing very little and seeing quite a lot. A good breakfast, a little exploring, some time by the pool and somewhere interesting to have a drink as the evening begins.

If we had just three days to show someone around, this is how we would spend them. It is not a checklist of everything Singapore has to offer, but rather a relaxed introduction to a city that we think is best enjoyed at its own pace.

DAY ONE

Gardens, skyline and the city after dark

Botanic Gardens
Marina Bay
Supertrees

We would always start with a good hotel. Location makes a difference in Singapore, particularly when you only have a few days. The Hyatt on Scotts is a favourite of ours, particularly for its superb swimming pool and relaxed outdoor bar. It is also very conveniently positioned for exploring the city.

For something a little more contemporary, The Singapore EDITION on Cuscaden Road is another great option. The design is beautiful, the bars are excellent and the rooftop pool is a particularly good place to spend an afternoon.

Once you have settled in, head to the Botanic Gardens. It is a lovely way to begin a visit, offering a complete change of pace from the surrounding city. Give yourself around an hour to an hour and a half to wander through the gardens. There are beautiful stretches of greenery, lakes and plenty of places to stop for a photograph.

One of the things we particularly like about Singapore is how easily nature and the city sit alongside one another. You can be surrounded by greenery one minute and back among the skyscrapers the next.

Head back towards the hotel for lunch and, if the weather is behaving itself, spend a little time by the pool. There is no particular reason to fill every hour. Singapore can be hot and humid, and a slower afternoon is often a very welcome part of the day.

Later, make your way down to Merlion Park for a walk around Marina Bay. The views across the water towards Marina Bay Sands are among the city's most recognisable, and it is particularly enjoyable as the light begins to soften.

For sundowners, head up to Spago or LAVO at Marina Bay Sands. Both offer wonderful views over the city, and there are few better places to settle in with a drink as the sun goes down.

Once darkness falls, make your way across to Gardens by the Bay and the Supertrees. They are impressive during the day, but really come into their own after dark. The illuminated structures and evening light show create quite a spectacle.

It is a memorable way to finish your first day and a good introduction to the contrast that makes Singapore so interesting: beautifully maintained green spaces, striking architecture and a city that seems to come alive in the evening.

DAY TWO

A change of scene and a taste of Singapore

Sentosa
Satay Street
Amoy Street
Duxton Hill

After a day exploring the city, we would spend the second day doing something completely different. Sentosa is only a short journey away, but it feels like a little escape from the city centre.

There are plenty of attractions on the island, but our preference would be to keep things simple and head straight to Tanjong Beach Club.

Settle in for lunch, enjoy the beach atmosphere and spend the afternoon in the sunshine. It is a lovely way to slow things down, particularly if you are travelling with family. There is no need to over-plan this one. A good lunch, a few hours by the beach and perhaps a swim are more than enough.

One of Singapore's real strengths is the variety it offers within such a small area. You can have a morning surrounded by skyscrapers, spend the afternoon beside the sea and still be back in the heart of the city in time for dinner.

Get back to the hotel before dark, take a little time to freshen up and then head out again.

As the evening begins, make your way towards Satay Street. There is something wonderfully informal about eating satay outdoors, with the smell of the grills in the air and the city moving around you. It is one of those experiences that feels distinctly Singaporean.

From there, head towards Amoy Street or Duxton Hill for dinner. These neighbourhoods are full of character, with restaurants, bars and plenty of interesting places tucked into the surrounding streets.

Singapore's food scene is one of the things we enjoy most about living here. You can eat incredibly well at almost any price point, from traditional local food to some genuinely excellent restaurants serving cuisines from all over the world.

We would recommend booking ahead for dinner, particularly at the more popular places. The good restaurants can fill up quickly, and it is much nicer to have somewhere arranged than spend the evening searching for a table.

DAY THREE

A little indulgence before heading home

Pool
Raffles
Changi

By the third day, we would be resisting the temptation to squeeze in another collection of attractions. One of the nicest things about Singapore is that it is a city where you can simply enjoy being somewhere.

Have a leisurely breakfast, spend some time around the pool and, if time allows, book yourself a massage. There is no need to make the final morning feel like another day of sightseeing.

Before heading to the airport, make one final stop at Raffles Hotel. It is one of Singapore's most recognisable landmarks, and its beautifully restored architecture makes it worth visiting in its own right.

Head into the Long Bar for a Singapore Sling if you fancy experiencing a little piece of the city's history. Alternatively, if the weather is good, settle into the sunny courtyard for a quieter drink.

There is something rather nice about finishing a short visit here. It is a little more traditional than the modern skyline that has become Singapore's signature, and a reminder of the different influences that have shaped the city over the years.

Finally, make your way to Changi. Even the airport deserves a little attention in Singapore. If you have enough time before your flight, Jewel is well worth a look, with its enormous indoor waterfall and landscaped gardens.

Getting around Singapore is easy, but there are a few car journeys in this itinerary. Download the Grab app before you arrive. It is the simplest way to arrange most journeys, and makes getting around incredibly straightforward.

THE EXPAT EDIT

Don't try to see everything. Singapore is better when you leave yourself time for a pool, a long lunch and a drink at sunset. The best way to experience the city is to enjoy a little of everything, without ever feeling in a hurry.
