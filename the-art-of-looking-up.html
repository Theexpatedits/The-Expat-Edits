THE JOURNAL

The Art of Looking Up

Murals, neon and the little details that make a place memorable.

Some of the things we remember most about travelling are not always the places we set out to see. They are the things we happen to notice along the way: a huge mural covering the side of a building, a hand-painted shopfront, a curious sculpture or a few words glowing above a restaurant doorway.

I have always enjoyed looking beyond the obvious. A walk through a neighbourhood can become its own little gallery, with something unexpected around the next corner. Sometimes it is beautiful, sometimes funny, occasionally a little strange — and often it says something about the character of the place.

This is a collection of those moments from our travels: street art, graffiti, restaurant signs, painted walls and details that made us stop, look twice and reach for the camera.

01 — THE STREET GALLERY

Art without an entrance fee

Painted walls
Unexpected corners
Everyday streets

There is something particularly satisfying about finding art where you were not expecting it. No gallery ticket, no carefully arranged exhibition and no sign telling you what to think. Just a building, a wall or a shopfront that someone has decided to turn into something more interesting.

The scale can be extraordinary. A painted face might stretch across several floors, while a smaller piece can transform an otherwise ordinary passageway. Some works are polished and deliberate; others feel spontaneous, layered over time by different hands and ideas.

I like that contrast. The city keeps moving around the artwork, people go about their day and the pictures become part of the street rather than something separated from it.

BIGGER THAN THE WALL

Portraits
Characters
A different point of view

The pieces that stay with me are often the ones that change the way a street feels. A giant portrait can give a blank wall a personality. A character painted into a building can make you smile as you walk past. And every now and then, the artist finds a way to use the shape of the building itself as part of the work.

It is a reminder that creativity does not need a traditional canvas. The city provides plenty of surfaces; all it takes is someone with an idea and the confidence to put it out there.

THE HUMAN TOUCH

Faces and expressions
Colour and character
Art in unexpected places

Faces seem to be a recurring theme in the things I photograph. Perhaps it is because a portrait gives a wall an immediate focal point, or perhaps because the expressions and colours can be so compelling. Either way, I find myself stopping for them more often than not.

Some are almost photographic, others deliberately exaggerated. A few are beautiful, a few slightly unsettling, and some seem to have a personality all of their own.

That is part of the appeal: there is no single style or rule. You simply take in what is in front of you and enjoy the fact that somebody has made an ordinary place a little less ordinary.

02 — WORDS IN THE NIGHT

A little light and a lot of attitude

Neon signs
Restaurant walls
Words worth a second look

Not all the art we come across is painted onto a wall. Some of it glows above a doorway or appears in the middle of a restaurant, doing its best to compete with the music, the menus and everything else going on around it.

Neon has a particular charm. It can be playful, nostalgic, a little provocative or completely nonsensical. During the day it might be easy to miss, but after dark it becomes part of the atmosphere of a neighbourhood.

I am drawn to the signs that have a sense of humour or a message that feels just right for the place. They are small details, but they give a restaurant or bar a personality before you have even sat down.

SAY IT IN LIGHTS

Music
Humour
Late-night energy

Some signs are almost impossible not to photograph. They are direct, occasionally irreverent and often better than any conventional piece of decoration.

Others work because of where they appear: a washroom, a bar, a shop window or a restaurant tucked into a busy street. The setting becomes part of the joke, and the words become part of the memory.

THE DETAILS THAT STAY

A phrase
A sign
A little local character

Perhaps that is why I enjoy collecting these images. They are not necessarily the grandest or most important things in a place, but they are often the details that bring back a particular feeling later on.

A name above a street, a phrase in a window or a sign that makes you laugh can become part of the way you remember a neighbourhood. They are little visual bookmarks from the trip.

03 — ART IN THE EVERYDAY

The places between the places

Restaurants
Interiors
Sculptures and surprises

Some of my favourite finds are not really street art at all. They are the unexpected objects, installations and decorative details that make a restaurant, hotel or public space feel distinctive.

A sculptural piece in a garden, an unusual figure watching over a dining room or a deliberately quirky bathroom can all have the same effect: they make you pause and take a closer look.

These are the things that remind me that good design does not always need to be serious. Sometimes it can simply be fun.

A SENSE OF PLAY

Unexpected details
A little theatre
Places with personality

The best spaces often have something that catches you off guard. It might be a piece of artwork that seems slightly out of place, an object that makes no obvious sense or a clever detail that you only notice once you have settled in.

I like places that are not afraid to have a bit of character. They feel more memorable than somewhere that has been designed to look exactly like everywhere else.

BEYOND THE WALLS

Public spaces
Beachside details
Art and design

And sometimes the most interesting details are found outside the city altogether. A hand-painted sign on a beach, a sculpture reflected in water or a small piece of lettering can be just as effective as a huge mural.

They are reminders to keep looking, even when there is no particular destination in mind. The camera often comes out for something that was never on the itinerary.
